### Boiler, Turbine and overall efficiency
##### Boiler Efficiency (~80–90%)
- Boiler efficiency measures how effectively the heat from coal combustion is transferred to the steam.
- Losses:
    - Flue gas losses
    - Unburnt carbon
    - Radiation & convection losses
- In modern plants, 80–90% is normal.  
    This is a heat transfer efficiency, not a power conversion efficiency.
---
##### Turbine Efficiency (actually 80–90%, not 30–40%)

- Turbine (isentropic) efficiency is how well the turbine converts steam energy (enthalpy drop) into mechanical work.
- Modern turbines: 80–90% efficient (aerodynamic/mechanical losses reduce from 100% to ~85%).
- 30–40% is NOT the turbine efficiency; it’s the overall plant (Rankine cycle) efficiency.
---
##### Overall Cycle Efficiency (30–40%)
- This is where the 30–40% figure comes from.
- It includes:
    - Boiler efficiency (~85%)
    - Turbine efficiency (~85%)
    - Condenser losses (major)
    - Auxiliary consumption (pumps, fans, etc.)
    - Generator losses
- After combining all these, a typical coal plant delivers **~35–38% net efficiency**.
---
##### 🔥 Energy flow (for 100 units of coal heat):

|Stage|Efficiency|Output (approx)|
|---|---|---|
|Boiler|85%|85 units steam|
|Turbine|85%|72 units mech.|
|Generator & Aux losses|~95%|68 units elec.|
|Condenser rejection|—|~32 units lost|
|**Net Plant Efficiency**|—|**~35–38%**|

---
##### So
- **Boiler efficiency = 80–90%**
- **Turbine efficiency = 80–90%**
- **Overall plant efficiency (Rankine cycle) = 30–40%**
