The turbine control/governing valve regulates the amount of steam admitted to the turbine.
**THE VALVE THROTTLING LOSSES IS THE ENTROY LOSS, NOT THE MW LOSS, BECAUSE MW LOSS IS HAPPENING WITH STEAM FLOW REDUCTION.**

When the valve is throttled:

- steam flow reduces
- pressure downstream of the valve falls
- boiler-side pressure tends to rise because steam generation temporarily exceeds turbine admission
- turbine output/MW reduces rapidly

This is useful for:

- load control
- frequency response
- rapid MW reduction

But the valve creates a thermodynamic penalty.

The pressure drop across the valve is a **throttling process**:

- enthalpy approximately constant
- entropy increases
- no useful shaft work is extracted in the valve itself

Therefore, some of the work potential that could have been extracted by allowing the pressure drop to occur through the turbine is lost.

The energy is not destroyed.

Instead, compared with an ideal case, more thermodynamic energy remains to be rejected later and less is converted into useful turbine work.

The steam may also become somewhat cooler after throttling, but this should **not** be explained as the fluid "using up energy to lower its temperature."

Instead:

> **The pressure changed, so the thermodynamic state corresponding to approximately the same enthalpy changed.**

Also, a pressure drop does **not automatically mean that steam becomes wet steam**.

If the state remains to the right of the saturation dome:

> **superheated steam → less-superheated steam**

Only if the new state crosses into the dome does liquid-vapor mixture appear.
