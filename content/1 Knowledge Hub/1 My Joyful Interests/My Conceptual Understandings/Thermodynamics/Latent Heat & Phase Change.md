A substance can absorb or reject a large amount of energy without its temperature changing significantly.

This happens during a phase change.

For example, at a fixed pressure:

**liquid water → vapor**

can absorb a large amount of heat while remaining around the same saturation temperature.

The added energy is going primarily into the **change of phase** rather than increasing temperature.

This explains:

- boiling
- evaporation
- condensation
- refrigeration
- steam generation

The same idea appears in the AC evaporator:

> Room heat enters the refrigerant and causes more of the refrigerant to evaporate.

And in the condenser:

> Heat leaves the refrigerant and causes vapor to condense into liquid.

A useful mental distinction:

> **Sensible heating → temperature changes.**

> **Latent heating → phase changes.**
