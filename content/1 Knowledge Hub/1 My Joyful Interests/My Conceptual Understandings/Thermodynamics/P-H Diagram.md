The pressure-enthalpy diagram is particularly useful for understanding refrigeration.

Vertical axis:

**Pressure**

Horizontal axis:

**Specific enthalpy**

The saturation dome separates the major regions:

- Left of dome → liquid region
- Inside dome → liquid + vapor
- Right of dome → superheated vapor

The four main refrigeration processes can be visualised as:

### Compressor

Pressure increases and enthalpy increases.

### Condenser

Heat leaves the refrigerant and enthalpy decreases.

### Expansion valve

Pressure drops while enthalpy remains approximately constant.

This is why the expansion process is represented approximately as a **vertical movement** on a P-H diagram.

### Evaporator

Heat enters the refrigerant and enthalpy increases.

---

### The key question when using the diagram

After any process, ask:

> **At the new pressure and enthalpy, where is the state relative to the saturation dome?**

That immediately tells you whether the fluid is:

- liquid
- two-phase mixture
- saturated vapor
- superheated vapor

This same way of thinking can also be applied to steam systems.
