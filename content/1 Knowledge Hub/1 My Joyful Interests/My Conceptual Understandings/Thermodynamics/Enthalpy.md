Enthalpy is a **thermodynamic state property** used especially conveniently for flowing fluids.

It is related to the internal energy of the substance and the pressure-volume contribution associated with a flowing fluid.

The important intuitive point is:

> **Enthalpy is an energy-related property of the state, not simply another word for heat.**

Heat is energy transferred between systems.

Enthalpy is a property describing the condition of the fluid.

This distinction is important in power plants.

---

### Why enthalpy is so useful in thermal plants

A working fluid can exist as:

- liquid
- saturated liquid
- liquid-vapor mixture
- saturated vapor
- superheated vapor

Enthalpy can be used to describe the state in **all of these regions**.

This makes it extremely convenient for analyzing:

- boilers
- turbines
- condensers
- pumps
- heaters
- refrigeration systems
- expansion devices

A particularly useful way to think about it is:

> **Heat entering a flowing fluid generally increases its enthalpy.**

> **Heat leaving a flowing fluid generally decreases its enthalpy.**

> **Work supplied to a flowing fluid can increase its enthalpy.**

> **A throttling process can change pressure and temperature while leaving enthalpy approximately unchanged.**

This is why enthalpy became so central in understanding both the AC and the steam turbine control valve.
